# Comparing with Adjectives

Adjectives describe people, animals, and things. A **comparative** compares two; a **superlative** picks one from a group of three or more.

## Comparative Adjectives: Two Things

Use `comparative adjective + than` to compare two things.

- A tiger is bigger than a rabbit.
- Dolphins are cleverer than monkeys.

## Superlative Adjectives: A Group

Use `the + superlative adjective` to say that one person or thing has the highest degree of a quality in a group. The group can be named or understood from the conversation.

- The blue whale is the biggest animal in the world.
- This lizard is the smallest animal here.

### Short Adjectives: **the + adjective-est**

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| quick | quicker | the quickest |
| small | smaller | the smallest |
| long | longer | the longest |
| loud | louder | the loudest |
| nice | nicer | the nicest |
| big | bigger | the biggest |
| happy | happier | the happiest |

Spelling changes to notice:

- Usually add **-est**: `quick → the quickest`; `long → the longest`.
- If the adjective ends in **-e**, add **-st**: `nice → the nicest`.
- Sometimes double the final consonant: `big → the biggest`.
- If a consonant comes before **-y**, change **y** to **i** and add **-est**: `happy → the happiest`.

Some two-syllable adjectives also take **-est**: `clever → the cleverest`.

### Longer Adjectives: **the most + adjective**

Many longer adjectives use **the most**. Do not add **-est** to these examples.

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| exciting | more exciting | the most exciting |
| beautiful | more beautiful | the most beautiful |
| boring | more boring | the most boring |

- I think tigers are the most exciting animals.
- I think dolphins are the most beautiful animals.

### Irregular Adjective: **good**

`good → better → the best`

- This book is better than that book.
- I think dolphins are the best animals.

## Compare Two or Compare a Group?

- **Two:** A tiger is bigger than a rabbit.
- **A group:** The blue whale is the biggest animal in the world.

In **the + superlative + noun** pattern, a singular subject takes **is** and a singular noun: `The tiger is the most beautiful animal.` A plural subject takes **are** and a plural noun: `Tigers are the most beautiful animals.` These are opinions, not facts everyone must agree on.
