# Tenses

This guide introduces simple English tenses for a beginner learner. A tense tells us when something happens.

## 1. Simple Present Tense

Use the simple present for things that are true, habits, and actions that happen often.

### With **be**

- I am seven years old.
- He is happy.
- They are at school.

Negative sentences:

- I am not tired.
- She is not sad.
- We are not late.

Questions:

- Are you ready?
- Is he at home?

### With action verbs

- I walk to school every day.
- We play after school.
- He walks to school every day.
- She plays after school.

> With **he**, **she**, or **it**, usually add **-s** or **-es** to the verb: `runs`, `watches`, `goes`.

Negative sentences:

- I do not like milk.
- He does not like milk.

Questions:

- Do you like apples?
- Does she like apples?

> After **do**, **does**, **do not**, or **does not**, use the base verb: `Does she like...?`, not `Does she likes...?`

## 2. Present Continuous Tense

Use the present continuous for an action that is happening **now** or around now.

**Pattern:** `noun/pronoun + am/is/are + verb-ing`

- I am eating now.
- She is reading a book.
- The dog is sleeping.
- We are learning English.
- They are playing outside.

Negative sentences:

- I am not eating.
- He is not sleeping.
- They are not playing.

Questions:

- Are you eating?
- Is she reading?
- Are they playing?

Short answers:

- Yes, I am. / No, I am not.
- Yes, she is. / No, she is not.
- Yes, they are. / No, they are not.

Compare:

- I eat breakfast every day. (a habit)
- I am eating breakfast now. (happening now)

> **Be** is necessary in the present continuous: say `I am eating`, not `I eating`.

Do not confuse these two patterns:

- I am swimming. (**be + verb-ing**: the action is happening now)
- I like swimming. (**verb + verb-ing**: swimming is an activity I like)

## 3. Simple Past Tense

Use the simple past for something that happened and finished before now.

Time words such as **yesterday**, **last night**, and **last week** often tell us to use the past tense.

### With **be**

- I was tired yesterday.
- She was at home.
- We were happy.
- They were at school.

> Use **was** with I/he/she/it. Use **were** with you/we/they.

Negative sentences:

- He was not tired.
- They were not late.

Questions:

- Was she at home?
- Were they happy?

### With action verbs

For many verbs, add **-ed**:

- I played football yesterday.
- She walked to school.
- We watched a film last night.

Some past-tense verbs are different and need to be learned:

| Present | Past |
| --- | --- |
| go | went |
| eat | ate |
| see | saw |
| have | had |
| make | made |
| come | came |

- We went to the park yesterday.
- I ate an apple.
- She saw a bird.

Negative sentences:

- I did not play football.
- She did not go to the park.

Questions:

- Did you play football?
- Did he go to school?

> After **did** or **did not**, use the base verb: `Did he go...?`, not `Did he went...?`

## 4. Present or Past?

| Simple present | Simple past |
| --- | --- |
| I play football every day. | I played football yesterday. |
| She walks to school. | She walked to school this morning. |
| We go to the park on Sundays. | We went to the park last Sunday. |
| He is happy today. | He was happy yesterday. |

## Quick Practice

Complete each sentence.

1. He ___ to school every day. (`walk` / `walks`)
2. We ___ football yesterday. (`play` / `played`)
3. They ___ at home last night. (`was` / `were`)
4. Look! The baby ___. (`is sleeping` / `sleeping`)

**Answers:** 1. walks; 2. played; 3. were; 4. is sleeping.
